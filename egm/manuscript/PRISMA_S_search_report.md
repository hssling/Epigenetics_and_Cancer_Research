# Search reporting — PRISMA-S checklist

Companion to the Methods section of *Epigenetic Approaches in Health and Public
Health Practice: An Evidence Gap Map*. Registered protocol: OSF Registries
[3xtf2](https://osf.io/3xtf2), DOI [10.17605/OSF.IO/3XTF2](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/3XTF2).

PRISMA-S (Rethlefsen et al., 2021) is the search-reporting extension to PRISMA
2020. Each of its 16 items is answered below. Where an item was **not** met, that
is stated as such rather than reworded into compliance.

---

## Information sources and methods

**1. Database name.** PubMed/MEDLINE, searched directly via the NCBI
E-utilities API (`esearch.fcgi`, `esummary.fcgi`, `efetch.fcgi`); and Europe PMC,
searched via the EBI REST `search` endpoint. Both are freely accessible and
require no institutional subscription or API key.

**2. Multi-database searching.** Two databases were searched. The registered
protocol committed to four (PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science Core
Collection, Cochrane CENTRAL). Embase and Web of Science were **not searched**:
the sole investigator has no institutional subscription to either. This is
recorded as an enacted amendment in `protocol/amendments.md` and is carried into
the manuscript's limitations, not into a claim of equivalent coverage.

**3. Study registries.** ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP are specified in the
protocol and have **not yet been searched**. They remain outstanding.

**4. Online resources and browsing.** None used. No hand-browsing of journal
tables of contents contributed records to the corpus at this stage; the
protocol's hand-search of the three highest-yield journals is triggered *after*
screening and has therefore not occurred.

**5. Citation searching.** Forward and backward citation chasing is specified in
the protocol on all included studies. As no study has yet been included, no
citation searching has been performed.

**6. Contacts.** No authors or organisations were contacted.

**7. Other methods.** None.

## Search strategies

**8. Full search strategies.** Reproduced verbatim, for every database, in
`search/query_executed.txt` (PubMed) and in the manifest of
`search/raw/europepmc_corpus.json` (Europe PMC). Both strings are generated by
code — `pipeline/query.py::build_pubmed_query` and
`pipeline/europepmc.py::build_europepmc_query` — so the published string and the
executed string cannot drift apart.

The two strategies are *not* line-by-line translations of one another, and the
difference is material:

- PubMed's strategy uses controlled vocabulary (`[MeSH]`), publication-type
  filters (`[pt]`), and title/abstract field tags (`[tiab]`).
- Europe PMC supports none of those with equivalent semantics. Its default
  free-text search runs against **full text** for open-access records, not
  title and abstract. Executed unrestricted, the same concept set returns
  239,868 records against PubMed's 9,089. Every term was therefore wrapped as
  `(TITLE:term OR ABSTRACT:term)` to approximate `[tiab]`, which returns 5,982.

**9. Limits and restrictions.** Date limit 2015-01-01 to 2026-12-31, applied in
both databases (`[dp]` in PubMed, `PUB_YEAR:[2015 TO 2026]` in Europe PMC).
Rationale is pre-specified in protocol Section 3.5: the window brackets the era
of array-based EWAS and validated epigenetic clocks. English-language
restriction is applied at **screening**, not in the search string, so that the
number of records excluded for language is countable and reportable.

**10. Search filters.** No published or named search filter was used. The
primary-study design filter and the review/editorial/letter exclusion were
written for this review and are reproduced in full in the query string. They
have not been externally validated.

**11. Prior work.** No search strategy was adapted from a previous review.

**12. Updates.** The searches reported here are the initial execution. PubMed was
executed 2026-09-13; Europe PMC 2026-09-14. No update search has been run. The
protocol commits to re-running both before final analysis if more than twelve
months elapse.

**13. Dates of searches.** PubMed: 2026-09-13. Europe PMC: 2026-09-14.

## Peer review

**14. Peer review.** **Not met.** The protocol commits to peer review of the
search strategy against the PRESS 2015 checklist "by an information specialist or
librarian where available". No information specialist or librarian was available
to this sole investigator, and no PRESS peer review has been conducted. This is a
genuine methodological shortfall and is reported as one. A self-assessment
against PRESS is not a substitute and is not claimed as one.

## Managing records

**15. Total records.** Reported in the PRISMA flow (`search/prisma_counts.csv`
and `search/prisma_counts_combined_v2.csv`) and in
`manuscript/PRISMA_flow.md`. Per-database counts, the union, and duplicates
removed by each rule are given separately rather than as a single total.

**16. Deduplication.** Automated, in `pipeline/dedup.py`, applied in order: exact
normalised DOI, then PMID, then fuzzy title match (rapidfuzz ratio >= 95)
additionally requiring the same first-author surname and a compatible publication
year. Two records that both carry an identifier and disagree on it are never
merged, however similar their titles.

One deviation from the registered rule was enacted (`protocol/amendments.md`,
2026-09-14): the publication-year comparison is relaxed to a +/-2-year window when
one record of a candidate pair is a preprint, because a preprint necessarily
predates its journal publication and the exact-year rule was double-counting the
same study. Exact-year matching is retained for all other pairs.

No manual deduplication was performed; every merge is recorded in the surviving
record's `merged_from` field and is therefore auditable record by record.
