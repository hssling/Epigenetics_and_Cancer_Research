## 1. Background and rationale

Epigenetic mechanisms sit at the interface between modifiable exposure and health outcome. They are, in principle, the molecular substrate through which nutrition, physical activity, tobacco, pollution, and psychosocial stress translate into disease risk — and, being reversible, they are in principle modifiable. A parallel literature uses epigenetic state as a *measurement*: methylation biomarkers for cancer screening, epigenetic clocks for risk stratification, cfDNA methylation for prognosis.

Despite two decades of work, there is no map of which of these approaches are actually ready for health or public health practice, and where in the care pathway they apply. Practitioners cannot tell dense evidence from thin, or mechanistic plausibility from demonstrated benefit.

This project supersedes two earlier, quarantined attempts in this repository, neither of which is extended. The root pipeline (`scripts/`, `output/Epigenetics_PublicHealth_Manuscript.md`) produced a manuscript whose reported findings are not derived from data: `proportion_positive`, `sensitivity`, and `specificity` were generated by `random.random()` and `random.uniform()`; 424 of 616 effect sizes (69%) are the hardcoded constant `0.3`; 468 of 616 sample sizes (76%) are the constant `200`; confidence intervals were computed as `effect x 0.8` to `effect x 1.2` with no variance input; and `data/prisma_counts.csv` records 616 identified, 0 excluded, 616 included — no screening occurred. The `re_research_2025/` module uses real, verifiable data (29 records, all reconciling arithmetically) but is described as a systematic review when it is an unscreened bibliometric snapshot, and its keyword classifier is first-match and misclassifies records. Neither output was ever submitted, shared, or cited; remediation is housekeeping, not formal correction.

The failure mode is recorded here because this protocol is shaped around preventing its recurrence. The root cause was not carelessness but an inverted dependency: the prior pipeline committed to producing a "network meta-analysis" as an output format, then substituted placeholder values whenever the source data could not fill that format. This protocol's data-integrity rules and its pre-specified, gated approach to synthesis (Section 9 below) are the structural fix.

## 2. Objectives

**Primary.** To map the available human evidence on epigenetic approaches — both interventions acting through the epigenome and epigenetic measurements guiding care — across the full continuum of health and public health practice, identifying where evidence is dense, where it is sparse, and where it is methodologically trustworthy.

**Secondary.**

1. To quantify the completeness of the causal chain in the existing literature (Section 3.2), establishing how much evidence connects intervention to epigenetic change *and* to health outcome.
2. To pool effect estimates in any cell of the map meeting pre-specified poolability criteria (Section 9).
3. To produce a research-priority statement derived from mapped absence, not from narrative assertion.

## 3. Eligibility criteria

### 3.1 Inclusion criteria

| Dimension | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Population | Humans, any age, any condition, any country |
| Conditions | All health conditions (disease-agnostic; organised by approach, not by disease) |
| Epigenetic marker | **Must be directly measured.** DNA methylation (global, candidate-gene, or epigenome-wide), histone modification, chromatin state, or epigenetic clock / DNAm age |
| Approach | A modifiable exposure or intervention, **or** an epigenetic measurement used to inform a clinical or public health decision |
| Design | Primary studies: RCT, non-randomised trial, cohort, case-control, cross-sectional |
| Date | 2015-01-01 to 2026-12-31 |
| Language | English |

### 3.2 The chain-completeness requirement

Every claim in this field is a three-link chain:

```
Exposure / Intervention  -->  Epigenetic change  -->  Health outcome
         (A)                        (B)                     (C)
```

Studies rarely measure all three. Eligibility requires a measured epigenetic marker (link B), and **chain completeness is coded as a mapped variable**, not used as an exclusion:

- **A->B** — intervention or exposure shown to alter an epigenetic marker. Mechanistic. Abundant.
- **B->C** — epigenetic marker shown to predict or associate with a health outcome. Prognostic. Abundant.
- **A->B->C** — intervention alters the marker *and* that change relates to a health outcome. The only design supporting a claim that an epigenetic approach works. Expected to be rare.

Making this a mapped variable rather than a filter is a deliberate design decision. It preserves the mechanistic and prognostic literature as mapped context while making the translational gap the map's central, quantified finding. Gap maps exist to report absence as a result; this is the mechanism by which this protocol does so.

### 3.3 Exclusion criteria

| Dimension | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Study type | Animal, in vitro, or cell-line only studies |
| Publication type | Reviews, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, editorials, comments, letters, conference abstracts without data |
| Marker | No directly measured epigenetic marker (this excludes intervention trials assumed to act epigenetically without measurement) |
| Marker | Non-coding RNA as the sole epigenetic marker (see Section 3.4) |

### 3.4 Non-coding RNA exclusion

miRNA and lncRNA are excluded as sole eligible markers. Rationale:

1. **Definitional.** ncRNA regulation is post-transcriptional and not a heritable chromatin state; strict definitions of epigenetics exclude it.
2. **Empirical.** Including ncRNA raises the PubMed corpus from 11,965 to 21,528 records — a near-doubling of screening burden.
3. **Yield.** The majority of additional records are mechanistic cancer-biology studies expected to fail the A->B / B->C eligibility test.

This boundary is stated explicitly here and in the manuscript's limitations as a scoping decision, not an oversight. Studies measuring ncRNA *alongside* an eligible marker remain eligible on the strength of that eligible marker.

### 3.5 Date window rationale

2015-2026 brackets the era of mature array-based EWAS (Illumina 450K/EPIC) and validated epigenetic clocks. Earlier work is dominated by candidate-gene studies on obsolete platforms whose findings are largely non-replicable and non-comparable. The window reduces the PubMed corpus from 11,965 to 9,089.

## 4. Information sources and search strategy

### 4.1 Sources

**Bibliographic databases:** PubMed/MEDLINE (primary, via NCBI E-utilities), Embase, Web of Science Core Collection, Cochrane CENTRAL.

**Trial registries:** ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP. These serve a dual purpose — identifying unpublished and ongoing studies, and populating a "registered but unreported" overlay on the map that indicates where evidence is in flight.

**Supplementary:** forward and backward citation chasing on all included studies; hand-search of the three highest-yield journals identified after screening.

## 5. Screening procedure

The investigator is the sole human reviewer, working with AI assistance. The design allocates human attention to errors that are unrecoverable, and relies on workflow structure to catch errors that are.

**Stage 0 — Calibration.** 300 randomly selected records. The investigator screens all 300 blind; the AI screens the same 300 independently. Cohen's kappa computed, all disagreements inspected, eligibility criteria and AI prompt refined. Repeated until kappa >= 0.75. The final kappa is reported in the manuscript.

**Stage 1 — AI title/abstract screening** of the full corpus. Structured output per record: decision (`include` / `exclude` / `unclear`), reason, and confidence score. All `unclear` records route to the investigator.

**Stage 2 — Validation, weighted to false negatives.**

False positives are self-correcting: every AI-include proceeds to full text, where the investigator assesses it directly. The unrecoverable error is a false negative — an eligible study excluded at title/abstract and never seen again.

Validation therefore targets AI-**excludes**: a 1,000-record sample, stratified by AI confidence to oversample low-confidence exclusions, hand-screened blind by the investigator.

This yields a quantified recall estimate with a confidence interval. If zero false negatives are found in 1,000, the exact (Clopper-Pearson) two-sided 95% upper bound on the false-negative rate is 0.368% — equivalently, a one-sided 95% upper bound of 0.299%. Against an expected 10,500-14,500 exclusions this supports a published statement that at most approximately 39-53 eligible studies were plausibly missed (two-sided bound; 31-43 one-sided). The manuscript reports the two-sided bound as the conservative figure.

If false negatives *are* found, the observed rate and its exact CI are reported as-is, and the sample is extended to tighten the estimate. **A recall estimate that comes out poor is reported, not re-run until favourable.**

This is a stronger and more auditable transparency claim than conventional dual-human review, which typically does not quantify its own recall at all.

Reported screening metrics: Cohen's kappa (calibration set), sensitivity/recall with 95% CI, specificity, and the full confusion matrix.

**Stage 3 — Full text.** Burden is split by deliverable:

- *Gap-map coding:* AI extracts the coded fields from full text. The investigator verifies a stratified sample plus every study in any cell driving a headline claim. This is accepted EGM practice — the task is assigning seven categorical fields, not extracting outcome data.
- *Meta-analysis extraction:* full manual extraction by the investigator, without exception. This set is expected to be 30-60 studies. Pooled estimates are where extraction error causes real harm.

**Reproducibility.** All AI prompts, model identifiers, versions, and run timestamps are committed as artefacts.

## 6. Data items and coding schema

One row per study. Dimensions are stored independently of any display format.

| Field | Values / type |
|---|---|
| `study_id`, `pmid`, `doi` | identifiers |
| `approach` | one of the 10 row categories |
| `care_stage` | one of the 7 care stages |
| `condition_domain` | one of the 8 condition domains |
| `modality` | intervention-effect / diagnostic-accuracy / prognostic |
| `chain_link` | A->B / B->C / A->B->C |
| `marker_class` | methylation (global / candidate / EWAS) / histone / chromatin / clock |
| `design` | RCT / non-randomised trial / cohort / case-control / cross-sectional |
| `n`, `population`, `country`, `follow_up` | nullable |
| `effect_metric`, `effect_value`, `effect_variance` | **nullable — no defaults** |
| `rob_tool`, `rob_domain_*`, `rob_overall` | per modality |

No default values are substituted for missing data: missing data stays `NULL` and propagates as `NULL`, enforced in code (this is the specific corrective for the prior pipeline's `effect = 0.3`, `population = 200` placeholder constants).

## 7. Risk of bias assessment

Tool selected by coded `modality`:

| Modality | Tool |
|---|---|
| Randomised interventions | Cochrane RoB 2 |
| Non-randomised interventions | ROBINS-I |
| Diagnostic accuracy | QUADAS-2 |
| Prognostic models | PROBAST |
| Certainty of pooled findings | GRADE (meta-analysis only) |

**Epigenetic methods quality checklist**, applied to every included study. Standard risk-of-bias tools do not capture epigenetics-specific validity threats:

1. Cell-type composition adjusted for, or purified cell populations used?
2. Batch effects corrected, and the correction stated?
3. Multiple-testing correction appropriate to the platform?
4. Independent replication cohort?
5. Tissue relevance justified (is the sampled tissue a defensible proxy for the target tissue)?
6. Platform and normalisation pipeline reported?

Unadjusted cell composition is the field's classic confounder: an apparent methylation difference in blood that is in fact a shift in leukocyte proportions. Mapping this lets the map distinguish where evidence exists from where it is trustworthy.

All six items are stored as **separate flags**, and a **composite tier** is derived from them. Flags support transparency and reader re-weighting; the composite drives cell colour in the map. Both are retained.

## 8. Synthesis and the poolability rule

Pre-specified here, in the protocol; applied only after coding is complete.

> A cell is poolable if it contains **>= 10 studies** that share a **modality**, have a **comparable comparator**, report an **extractable common effect metric with variance**, and are **not uniformly at high risk of bias**.

Cells passing the gate are pooled as follows:

- **Intervention effects:** random-effects meta-analysis (REML) with Hartung-Knapp adjustment. Report I-squared and **prediction intervals** alongside confidence intervals. Egger's test for small-study effects where >= 10 studies. Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis.
- **Diagnostic accuracy:** bivariate random-effects or HSROC models. Naive independent pooling of sensitivity and specificity is not used.

**Network meta-analysis is not planned.** It will be undertaken only if a genuinely connected network with shared comparators emerges from the coded data, and only with formal transitivity assessment. Pre-committing against NMA is deliberate: the prior pipeline named the method before seeing the data and then manufactured data to suit it.

**If no cell passes the gate, this is reported as a pre-registered null finding.** That is a legitimate result. Deliverable 3 (the nested meta-analysis) is not promised in advance: this protocol pre-specifies the *rule* for identifying a poolable cell, not the cell itself.

## 9. Contingencies

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Corpus larger than estimated after multi-database dedup | Counts in Section 4.3 are measured, not estimated. If dedup yields >20,000, tighten the date window to 2018-2026 as a pre-specified contingency recorded in this protocol |
| AI screening recall below acceptable threshold | Stage 0 gate (kappa >= 0.75) must pass before Stage 1 proceeds. If it cannot be reached after prompt refinement, scope narrows to the exposure arm only (4,029 records), which is single-reviewer feasible |
| No cell passes the meta-analysis feasibility gate | Pre-registered as a legitimate null. The gap map stands alone as deliverable 2 |
| Full-text screening burden exceeds solo capacity | Gap-map coding is AI-assisted with sampled verification (Section 5, Stage 3). Only the much smaller meta-analysis set requires full manual extraction |
| Provisional approach rows prove empty or over-broad | Confirmed during Stage 0 pilot; changes recorded in `amendments.md` rather than made silently |
| PROSPERO declines the EGM registration | OSF Registries fallback, specified in advance |

## 11. AI use declaration

An ICMJE-compliant statement in the Methods specifies precisely which tasks were AI-assisted, which model and version, and the measured validation metrics from Section 5.

**Tasks that are AI-assisted:**

- Title/abstract screening (Stage 1), gated by the calibration requirement in Section 5 (kappa >= 0.75) and validated against 1,000 hand-screened AI-excludes for false negatives (Section 5, Stage 2).
- Full-text field extraction for gap-map coding (Section 6), with investigator verification of a stratified sample plus every study in any cell driving a headline claim (Section 5, Stage 3).

**Tasks that remain fully manual:** full-text screening decisions themselves, all meta-analysis data extraction (Section 5, Stage 3), risk-of-bias judgements, and all interpretation, synthesis, and writing.
